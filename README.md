## 📖 My Journey

**From copy-paste to building open-source tools — a path that was never straight.**

In **2019**, I started with Unity and game development. It was so fascinating that I made a course on Faranesh, and hundreds of people signed up. Faranesh is gone now, but it taught me that teaching is itself a way of learning.

But let me be honest: back then I didn't really understand programming :) I often just copied things, or installed a ready-made package and told my family, "Look, I made a game!" That was me until my parents got me a private tutor. That tutor opened my eyes and turned me from a copy-paster into a real learner. My path shifted toward .NET, and I kept going for a year. Along the way, an internship at Arad, a knowledge-based company in Qom, gave me a different experience.

I was almost 16, and work felt so good that I wanted to drop out of school! Morning at school, afternoon to night at work. But my mom stopped me. And I'm glad I listened. That summer, a course at Sharif University with Professor Sharifi Zarchi opened a new path for me: I realized writing code alone isn't enough. I want proper thinking!

I took my senior year more seriously — not for the entrance exam, but to understand. That got me into a public university in Qom. Then I went back to coding. Since Python was very popular at university, I learned it well and in my third semester became a TA for one of my professors. Later, at an image-processing company, I did backend work with .NET and got familiar with gRPC and AI. In an IoT project, I got to know MQTT.

Then I realized something was missing! I knew backend, but I didn't really understand UI. I spent months starting from zero: raw HTML/CSS without AI, and an 80-hour JavaScript course with practice and repetition. Then I moved to Node.js, and since then my love for open-source projects has only grown.

Recently, I released [DocSite](https://github.com/SajjadSharifi/DocSite) on GitHub: with `.md` and `.ipynb` files, you can build a complete documentation site.

I'm also into psychology and personal development. I believe soft skills aren't just a "nice extra"; they're tools that help me listen better, understand better, and better help the people who trust me.

I learn every day — not for money. To get better. Because maybe what I learn today will help someone tomorrow. And that "maybe" is enough.

In **2025**, at a professor's request, I recorded a free Git course in Persian (Farsi), covering only the practical parts. I think it's a good start:  
👉 [startabad.com/Allcourses/git/](https://startabad.com/Allcourses/git/)
